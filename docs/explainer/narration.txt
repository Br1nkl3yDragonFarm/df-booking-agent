Welcome to the Dragon Farm booking hub. In the next few minutes you'll learn how to run the label from one dashboard: every tool, plus the AI team of agents and skills behind it.
Start every day in the Command Center. Unlock it with the team password, and enter the CRM passcode once on each device. The Today sheet shows follow-ups due, your next show, and anything that needs attention. When the badge in the corner says Synced, your phone and computer share the same list.
This is the dashboard overview, your booking home base. The top row counts leads, contacted, interested, and booked. Under Follow-ups due, tap Draft for a ready-made follow-up, Done when it's handled, or Snooze.
Find bookers runs a live web search for the people who book bands: venues, talent buyers, festivals, colleges, breweries and fairs. Set where, the act, and the dates you need to fill. Every result links to its source, so check the contact before you pitch. Save a search to run every Monday, and new places land in your pipeline automatically.
In the Email composer, start from a lead or fill in the details, pick a tone, and press Write the email. Claude drafts the pitch. Edit it, then Review and send, from booking at dragonfarm dot net, with a tracked press kit link. When they reply, the lead is flagged, and one tap drafts your answer.
The Lead tracker holds every venue and buyer you're talking to. Move each one from New, to Contacted, to Interested, to Booked, and give every lead a follow-up date. Leads near a booked show show the distance. Import a spreadsheet with Import CSV, and duplicates are skipped. A good rhythm: follow up after a week, then again in two, and stop after three touches.
The Calendar shows confirmed shows and holds month by month, and flags double bookings. Tap Subscribe to get one private link that puts every show on the band's phones and keeps it up to date.
Booked shows is where a yes becomes money. Set the deal and deposit, send an invoice with a card payment link, and send the contract as a link they sign on their phone. Fill nearby dates searches for gigs within a three-hour drive. You'll also find social post drafts, the fan QR code, and show-day settlement.
The Sync agent works the same way for TV, film, ads, and games. Find music supervisors and briefs, pitch with clean and instrumental versions ready, quote a fee with the sync kit, and keep a log of every pitch.
Brand deals finds sponsors that fit the band. Claude drafts a pitch that leads with fit and a content idea, and the deal check flags exposure-only pay, rights grabs, and forever exclusivity.
Press kits is the public page buyers open from every pitch. Add photos, music, video, past shows, and real fan numbers. Only list facts you can stand behind, because buyers check.
The Fan list builds an audience you own. Print a QR code sign for the merch table. Fans add their email and town, and you can email everyone, or just one town, with a ticket button. Send yourself a test first.
What's working shows which kinds of leads, towns, and sources turn into paid shows, what they pay, and which emails get answered. Let the numbers decide where you spend your time.
Captions turns a video into subtitles for YouTube or CapCut. It runs in your browser, free, and your files never leave your computer. Just check sung lyrics line by line.
Automations connects the hub to n8n. When a show is booked, a deposit is paid, or someone replies, it can post to Discord, log a Google Sheet, or buzz your phone. Use Send test to check your setup.
The public Shows page publishes booked shows for fans, with tickets, calendar links, and directions. It never shows money or contacts, and private events stay off.
Now meet your AI team: eight specialists inside Claude Code. SNOOP, the coordinator, splits big goals and hands them out. The booker, sync scout, and deal checker handle shows, placements, and contracts. The promoter, PR specialist, designer, and coder cover marketing, press, design, and the tools themselves.
You talk to them in plain English. Name the agent, like: have the deal checker read this agreement before I sign it. Or give SNOOP the whole goal: book a Southeast run in March and promote it.
Behind the agents are skills. SNOOP skills cover label operations, like venue booking, tour routing, and contract review. TUPAC skills cover social media and marketing, like captions, hooks, and ads. Agents load them automatically, or you can ask for one by name.
Here's a week with the hub. Monday, read the digest. Midweek, find bookers and send pitches, or have the booker draft them. Thursday, work your replies and run offers past the deal checker. When a show books, send the contract and invoice, fill nearby dates, and let the promoter sell tickets. Each month, check What's working.
And the golden rule: AI drafts, you approve. Nothing is sent, posted, signed, or paid without your OK. Now open dragonfarm dot net slash dashboard, and get booked.
